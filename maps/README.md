# Writing a map

A map is one JSON file in this directory. A server reads every `.json` here at boot (and again on `zz_reload`), checks it, and plays it; a file it refuses is listed by `zz_maps` with the reason, and every other map still plays. Clients never read these files: the server sends the map it is playing to each of them when the map changes.

The maps shipped here are generated by [`../tools/build_maps.py`](../tools/build_maps.py) and should be changed there, not here: `tools/build_maps.py --check` fails when a file is not what it would write. What follows is the format itself, which game-zombie-zone's `game/zz_map_doc.gd` validates.

Every length is metres, every angle degrees, every time seconds. A position is `[x, y, z]`, y is up, and the ground is at y 0.

## The document

```json
{
	"format": 1,
	"kind": "zombiezone_map",
	"id": "zz_my_map",
	"name": "My Map",
	"author": "you",
	"blurb": "One line, shown when the map starts.",
	"mode": "exodus",
	"survivors": 12,
	"next": "zz_my_map_2",
	"survive_seconds": 0,
	"kill_y": -20.0,
	"environment": {},
	"boxes": [],
	"props": [],
	"lights": [],
	"water": [],
	"spawns": {"survivors": []},
	"areas": {"start": [], "end": []},
	"route": [],
	"nav": {"spacing": 1.0, "clearance": 0.35, "floors": [], "links": []},
	"items": [],
	"objectives": [],
	"sleepers": [],
	"zombie_spawns": [],
	"posts": [],
	"caches": [],
	"barricades": [],
	"day_environment": {}
}
```

| Field | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `id` | Lower-case letters, digits and underscores. Unique on a server; a second file with the same id is refused. |
| `mode` | `exodus`: get from the start shelter to the end one, everybody standing (`campaign`, its old name, is still read). `survival`: hold out, the way the server's `zz_survival` says: ten waves by default, or days and nights, or a clock of `survive_seconds` (the server's `zz_survive_seconds` when 0). |
| `survivors` | The team the map is built for. Stand-ins fill to it when it is more than the server's own number; leave it out on a map four can play. |
| `next` | An exodus map's next map, by id. Empty hands over to the server's rotation and the map vote. |
| `kill_y` | Below this, a survivor is dead and a zombie is gone. |
| `world` | Optional, `zz_world_<id>`: the converted map's own drawn geometry, a pack of its own (`world/zz_world_<id>/`, written by `tools/build_worlds.py`) that a client fetches when its server is on this map. Drawn only: the boxes still collide and the horde's ground is still cut from them; without the pack the boxes are drawn. |
| `environment` | Sky, sun, ambient light and fog. Anything left out is the default daylight; the maps here are night, dusk and fog. |
| `ambient` | The creepy ambience under the map: a mood (`outdoor_night`, `indoor_hall`, `industrial`, `cave_tunnel`, `snow_wind`, `swamp`, `city_ruins`, `interior_horror`) or an object `{"mood", "indoor", "volume", "frequency", "extra"}`: `indoor` is the mood under a roof (the player crossfades into it), `volume` (0.25 to 2) and `frequency` (0 to 4) scale the player's and server's settings for this map, `extra` adds another mood's one-shots. Optional; without it ZzAudio votes from the sound names. `tools/ambient.py` authors and checks it. |

## What is solid: boxes

```json
{"at": [0, 1.5, 10], "size": [10, 3, 0.3], "mat": "brick", "tint": [0.8, 0.75, 0.7]}
```

`at` is the centre. `mat` is one of the game's surfaces (`asphalt`, `sidewalk`, `concrete`, `brick`, `plaster`, `paint`, `tile`, `tile_check`, `wood_floor`, `wood`, `metal`, `stone`, `grass`, `sand`, `water`, `ceiling` and more; see `zz_materials.gd`). Optional: `yaw`, `tint`, `"surface": "brick"` (what the box is made of, over what its `mat` draws: one of dot-map's surface kinds `concrete`, `concrete_clean`, `plaster`, `brick`, `tile_white`, `tile_dark`, `tile_checker`, `wood_floor`, `wood_planks`, `asphalt`, `metal`, `rust`, `corrugated`, `grass`, `ground` or an alias such as `road`; only the kinds the game vendors draw, the rest keep the `mat`'s look), `"solid": false` (seen, not hit: water), `"visible": false` (hit, not seen) with an optional `"kind": "clip" | "nodraw" | "sky"` saying what the hidden box is (a clip or nodraw box stops movers but not shots; a sky box collides with nothing), `"gate": "id"` (removed when an objective opens it) and `"nav"`.

`"solid_props": [[x, y, z, sx, sy, sz, yaw], ...]` (optional, at most 600) are the solid props of a converted map's drawn world as boxes (centre, size, degrees about Y), written by `tools/solid_props.sh` after `ship_docs.sh`. Both the server (which has no `world/`) and the client collide with them, and the horde's graph cuts out those over 0.3 m tall.

`"barricade": "id"` makes the box part of a barricade (see below).

`nav` says whether the zombies treat the box as something in the way. A floor, a ceiling or a roof is not; unsaid, anything whose top is at a nav floor's height is taken for a floor and anything else is in the way. Something overhead (a lintel over a door) is never in the way, because the zombies' ground is tested at their feet.

## What stands about: props

```json
{"id": "cars_sedan", "at": [-3.5, 0, 6], "yaw": 10}
```

By catalogue id, from game-zombie-zone's `props/catalogue.json` (Kenney's cars, street furniture, survival kit, graveyard kit, furniture and nature). `at` is the middle of its footprint on the floor. Anything taller than half a metre is in the zombies' way.

## Where the survivors are: spawns and areas

`spawns.survivors` is a list of `{"at", "yaw"}`, four at least; a fifth survivor laps round them. `areas.start` and `areas.end` are lists of `{"min", "max"}` boxes. The horde holds off while everybody is in a start area (for `zz_start_grace` seconds, or until somebody leaves it); a campaign map is won when every survivor still standing is in an end area. A survival map needs no end.

## Where the zombies are: nav, route, hints, sleepers

`nav.floors` is a list of `[x0, z0, x1, z1, y]` rectangles the zombies may stand on; the game lays a point every `spacing` metres over them and cuts out every box and prop in the way, `clearance` metres wide. **A floor must reach through a doorway**: two rooms whose floors stop at the wall either side of a door are two graphs, and a zombie in one never finds a survivor in the other. `nav.links` joins two points the floors cannot (a drop, a ladder), as `[from x, y, z, to x, y, z]`.

`route` is the way from the start to the end. The director spawns ahead of the survivors along it and behind them, never in sight. `zombie_spawns` are places a mob likes to come from (the far end of an alley, a doorway); `sleepers` are `[x, y, z, yaw]` where a sleeper sits, waiting for somebody to come too close.

## What lies about: items

```json
{"kind": "weapon", "id": "shotgun", "at": [-2.4, 0.82, -12.5]}
```

`kind` is `weapon`, `ammo`, `medkit`, `pills`, `throwable` or `box`. A weapon or throwable without an `id`, or with one the server does not have, is drawn from the server's weapons; a `box` without an `id` is a random type from the server's `boxes/boxes.json`. An ammo pile is never used up. Put an item on a floor or a table (`y` 0.82 is a Kenney table's top).

## What to do: objectives and gates

```json
{"id": "fence", "kind": "use", "at": [17.3, 1, 66], "label": "Cut the fence lock", "seconds": 3,
 "holdout": 35, "opens": ["fence"], "message": "The lock is screaming. Here they come!", "prop": "padlock"}
```

`use` is held (E) for `seconds`, then held out for `holdout` seconds while the horde comes (a panic, unless `"panic": false`), then every gate in `opens` is removed. `reach` is done by standing within `radius`. Objectives are played in order; the HUD shows the current one.

`prop` is what the objective is, drawn: `padlock` (a chain and padlock hung on the face of the gate it opens, nearest the objective, and gone with the gate), `lever` or `radio` (standing at `at`, turned by `yaw` degrees). Unsaid draws nothing; an unknown one is ignored.

A box may say how it is drawn with `look`: `chain_link` draws a see-through fence (posts, rails, mesh) instead of a solid box; its collision is still the box. An unknown look is drawn as the box.

A gate is any box with `"gate": "id"`. A shut gate is a wall to survivors, zombies and the zombies' ground alike; game-zombie-zone's `headless_maps` checks the end area cannot be reached while it is shut.

## Holding it: posts, caches, barricades, daylight

A survival map is held by a team, so it says where:

- `posts` (`[x, y, z]`, up to 64): where a team holds. Stand-ins share them out, fight from them, and mend the barricades near them.
- `caches` (`[x, y, z]`, up to 32): where supplies appear in every breather: ammunition, a medkit or pills, a gun that gets better as the waves go on, and from the third wave a throwable. `y` 0.82 puts them on a Kenney table.
- `barricades` (`[{"id", "label", "health"}]`): the doors and windows the horde breaks down and the team mends. Every box with `"barricade": "id"` is part of that barricade. **Give a barricade's boxes `"nav": false`**: the zombies must path straight into it to tear at it, and a barricade cut out of their ground is a window nothing ever comes to (`headless_maps` checks both of its faces are on the ground). `label` is how the team is told it fell ("They are through the west doors!"); `health` is its strength (the server's `zz_barricade_health` when left out). Planks to chest height let the team shoot over them; to the lintel, they do not.
- `day_environment`: the same fields as `environment`, for the days when the server holds survival by nights; `environment` is then the night.
