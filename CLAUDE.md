# zombie-zone-maps

The maps game-zombie-zone plays, as JSON documents. Read `../../CLAUDE.md` and `../game-zombie-zone/CLAUDE.md` first: the format is defined and validated by game-zombie-zone's `game/zz_map_doc.gd`, and `maps/README.md` is the mapper's copy of it.

**`maps/` is what the game reads**, through a dot-bootstrap link (`game-zombie-zone/.gitignore` names it). The game reads every `.json` there plus the practice street built into it; a refused document is listed by `zz_maps` with the reason, and every other map still plays.

**`tools/build_maps.py` writes every document, and the JSON is committed.** Edit the builder, run it, commit both. `--check` (CI) fails when a committed file differs from what the builder writes.

## A map is regions, and everything else follows from them

mg-prop-hunt-maps' builder places walls with their holes, one call per wall. This one does not, because a zombie map has a second floor plan that must agree with the first: the ground the zombies walk (`nav.floors`). Two lists kept by hand drift — a wall moved and a nav floor not is a zombie walking through the wall, and a doorway one floor short of the next room is a safe room no zombie ever enters. So a map here is a list of **regions** (rectangles a person can stand in) and the builder derives both from them: the floor, a wall on every edge of their union that faces nothing (`Map._walls`, found on a half-metre grid and merged into runs), a ceiling over an indoor region, and the nav floors, which are the regions.

- **Keep two regions that should not join half a metre apart**, and the gap fills with wall from both sides. A **door** is a region across that gap (`Map.door`); the builder puts a lintel over it up to the taller neighbour and stretches its nav floor 0.6 m into the rooms either side, because a door's own floor is half a metre deep and the graph would not cross it otherwise.
- **A wall reaches past a convex corner** by its own thickness so two walls meet, and never into ground somebody stands on (that is the L-shaped corner case; the extension is skipped where the next cell along the run is walkable).
- **A railing is a region with `rail=`**: the edges that face nothing are a 1.3 m wooden rail instead of a wall. 1.3 because a survivor jumps a metre; the pier is over water, and kill_y is below it.
- **Every region corner is on the half-metre grid**, and the builder stops on one that is not.

## Survival maps: facades, barricades, posts, caches

- **A building has a facade** (`room(..., facade=, facade_tint=)`): the outdoor region's wall facing it across the gap takes the building's face, height and colour, not the car park's fence (`_walls`' `face`, looking past the gap between two rooms too, or each room gap was a slot of fence). Opt-in, so the street maps are byte-for-byte what they were. An opening in a facade gets a two-half lintel (wall inside, facade outside) and a facade building a `roof` over its ceiling.
- **A barricade is `door(..., barricade=id, label=, strength=, boards=)`** across the gap: `window` planks to 1.5 m (the team shoots over; use it wherever the team must see the horde: the mall's entrances, the ballpark's tunnels, the camp's gates, which were steel shutters nobody could shoot through), `door` planks to the lintel, `shutter` steel. Its boxes are `"nav": false` so the horde paths into it.
- **`post`, `cache`, `survivors=`, `day=`** on every big survival map; `wall_lamp` over windows and doors outside, or a horde at the boards is invisible at night.
- Big maps take `spacing=1.25`–`1.5` (fifteen to eighteen thousand nav points; build 1–2 s at map start) and stay under 10 KB on the wire.
- `fabric` and `rubber` have a red base under a tint: coloured canvas and seats are `paint` or `flat`.
- **Every door on a survival map gets a nav link through it** (`survival_links`): the graph drops a door's own points within half a spacing of a room's, and the school's gym doors lost all theirs, so the hallway reached the gym round the outside (`headless_maps` now fails a post reached the long way round). The street maps are left without, byte-for-byte.
- **Nothing across a doorway at body height** (a board, a sign, a shelf): the floor plan does not know about it and a stand-in stands against it.

## What `headless_maps` asks of every map

game-zombie-zone's `examples/headless_maps.tscn` builds each map the way a server does and checks twelve things, the zombie half being the one a render cannot show: from the first spawn, the zombies' ground (with every gate open) reaches every route point, objective, sleeper, spawn hint and the end area; with the gates shut, the end area is cut off. Run it after any change here.

**It found the builder's first bug on its first run**: the door floor was stretched along the door's long side instead of across the gap, so the laundromat, the warehouse and both maps' end rooms were islands the horde could never enter. The start rooms joined by luck (their floors were within the dedupe distance of the street's). Both gates and the far rooms were "reachable" only once the stretch was across the gap.

## Converted Source maps (`tools/import_bsp.py`)

**Reading the map and cutting brushes into boxes are dot-map's** (`dot-map/tools/bsp`, see its README): this importer is the game's adapter on top, deciding what an entity and a material mean here. A reader or box fix goes into dot-map, where every converting game gets it; re-convert the maps after one and diff. `zs_ravenstreet.json` was left as converted on 2026-10-17 when the rest moved to the shared tool, because its committed lightmap is hashed against the document and the re-conversion differed by one box by 1 mm: re-convert it with its next bake.

**The importer must agree with the game's graph, not with its own cell flood.** `tools/nav_emulate.py` is dot-npc's `DotNpcNavBuilder` in Python (a point a metre apart, dropped within 0.35 m of an obstacle): a doorway a metre wide has no point in it, so a room the cell flood reached was an island to the horde (Raven Street's posts and caches failed `headless_maps` that way). Keep it in step with the builder. Posts go inside the houses (`post_region` in the spec) because posts on an open street leave the choke finder nothing to find. `drop_lids`/`drop_decor` remove the sky lid and distant scenery, whose walkable tops took the annotator's random routes off the ground; `roadblocks` pinch an open street so it has chokes (`headless_nav` fails a map with none on the horde's routes); `spawn_z_max` drops spawns pressed against a wall. Textures are never the author's; props map to Kenney ids by name (`prop_models`). A brush's surface comes from dot-map's shared `tools/bsp/dotbsp/source_textures.tsv` (a new texture goes there, never in this repository; `bsp.py unmapped <bsp>` lists what it misses); the importer's `GAME_MATERIAL` turns the list's generic ids into this game's (`wood_planks` is `wood`, `plaster_painted` is `paint`) and `OVERRIDES` keeps the two it knew better (road, pavement, glass). The list's tints are not used.

**The flood's jump/drop links run through thin walls and window sills, so the importer filters them** (`filter_wall_links`, `link_blocked`): a link whose line is blocked by a wall box at least 0.55 m tall is cut, unless it is the only join between two parts of the graph (then it stays), and a link into a pocket cut off by it is replaced by a doorway link (`doorway links`: islands under 150 points joined to the main ground by the shortest line a 0.25 m body walks clear of every wall). Islands of 40 points or more with no clear line keep their old link, because cutting them cut the horde off half of Jurassic Park. A survivor spawn whose entity has a `targetname` or `StartDisabled` carries `name` (64 characters) and `disabled` in the document, so the game's `spawn_enabled` can toggle it like the live entity (none of the shipped sources has one yet, so no document changed). Survivor spawns off the main ground are moved onto it (`survivor spawns: N moved`; dropping them fails `headless_maps`' team size check). Spec keys: `strict_wall_links` (opt-in, grow 0.25 and 0.4 m walls; it cut ground on Black Mesa and Raven Street, so it is off by default), `keep_wall_links` (opt out of the filter). On Atix the stand-in stuck share fell from 53.2% to 1.1%. `widen_doorways` (spec, metres; Nuke uses 1.7) trims the wall pieces flanking a gap narrower than two graph points, so rooms join by geometry instead of a link through the wall; the spec's own `extra_links` count as joins when the filter decides what to cut. Nuke now has no link through a wall, its doorway sill is `cut`, and its lightmap needs a rebake (hashed over the boxes). Still open: Atix's last pocket has a 0.75 m doorway the graph cannot cross.

**Stairs made of clip slabs** (Pathogen): `clip_underlay` leaves an invisible slab lying 0.02 to 0.4 m under a floor out of the graph (it grows the clearance upward and left the whole ground floor without a point), and `keep_treads` keeps the small boxes that are stair treads (a neighbour within 6 cm whose top differs by 0.1 to 0.45 m) past `drop_small` and out of the graph, so the stairs stay solid for bodies and the flights still join the levels. Together with `flood_step` 0.55, `link_dy` 1.0 and `nav_min_depth` 1 they lift the share of the original's own navigation area that the horde's main ground covers from 43.6% to 53.9%; the rest of the lab (the roof level's steepest flights, slope 0.66 against the graph's 0.5) is still apart. `keep_treads` also keeps a chain of three or more touching small boxes whose tops climb (a porch's seven 8 cm steps, 15 cm deep), then drops the ones no flood cell stands on and applies `max_boxes` again; with `nav_min_depth` 1 the steps hold graph points, which is what joined Biohazard Manor's porch (the flood's `flood_step` 0.3 stopped at it before). An invisible thin slab over 300 m2 above 3 m is a sky lid for `drop_lids` (the manor's clip slabs gave 1929 nav floors at y 3.7 and 79 KB on the wire); `auto_spec.py` now writes all three, so generated specs written before 2026-10-23 lack them.

**The flood samples a cell's centre, so a wall thinner than a cell is invisible to it** (Run For Your Life's dividers are 2 cm): the route, items and zombie hints were put on the far side of walls the body cannot pass, and `headless_maps` found the zombies' ground cut. A spec opts in with `thin_walls` (Run For Your Life); Nuke's route was authored against the old flood and its panes of glass and clip slivers would seal it, so it is left off until redone. Items and `zombie_start` points must be inside the walls' rooms, and `headless_maps` checks them against the graph. Every map needs `toys` hints (`[toy, anchor, dx, dz]`, anchors `spawn`, `["route", i]`, `["post", i]`).

**A converted map must keep its drawn world, and `tools/completeness.py --src <bsp dir>` measures it** (the share of the source's drawn face area lying on a box of the document; it fails a map under its spec's `min_coverage`, default 0.80, and a spec that crops on purpose or is mostly terrain or sea sets a lower one with `min_coverage_why`). Run it after every re-conversion. 4-Way Tunnel shipped with 11.7% of its world and every suite passed, because the graph, the route and the gates were all on the piece that was left. There were two causes. First, a `cut` called the east half a 3D skybox; it held 758 of the 1204 world brushes, the lights, the lifts and the weapons (a skybox has a `sky_camera` entity: look before cutting). Second, `drop_ramps` dropped every slab-cut brush rising 0.9 m, which is every curved tunnel wall, arch and leaning roof. **A walkable ramp or stair is never dropped**: the global world still draws it, so a dropped one is a floor the player falls through (4-Way's corridor ramps, 66 brushes, until 2026-10-23). `drop_ramps` alone now drops nothing; with `drop_every_ramp` (the mall, for the wire limit) it drops only slab-cut brushes no body can walk up (arches, leaning walls). The mall's committed document also predates drift in dot-map's texture list and the auto lights, so a re-conversion changes materials and light positions and needs a rebake.

**The boxes collide with everything the global world draws as world**: model 0 plus every brush entity whose dot-map class treatment is `world` (func_detail, func_wall, func_brush, func_breakable, func_physbox and the rest), not only the spec's `solid_classes`, and GRATE brushes (ladders, mesh fences) too. The game breaks nothing, so an upright breakable (func_breakable, func_breakable_surf, func_physbox: a door, a window) not in `solid_classes` stays open as before and only a flat one (a floor, a lid, 0.5 m or thinner) collides; the `barricade_class` is boarded, never boxed as well. **`tools/walk_check.sh [id ...]` is the walk test** (dot-map's `bsp.py audit --doc`): every player-solid brush of those owners must hold a box, and a 24-unit grid over every standable top must find ground under it; run it after every re-conversion. Brush movers (func_door, func_tracktrain, func_button) are drawn but collide only as gates or with the spec's mechanics.

**`drop_small` and `max_boxes` are a fallback, not a filter**: `import_bsp.py` converts the whole map first and keeps it when it fits the wire (58500 compressed bytes, 6000 boxes, 800 nav floors, 600 nav links); only when it does not, it drops small boxes, then puts back the largest of them as far as the budget goes, and applies `max_boxes` last. A map that still fails `walk_check.sh` after that (the mall, POTC, the manor, both krusty krabs, roy_highway, verystill) is over the budget, and collides with the world pack's hulls: when a budget pass dropped anything, the importer writes `world_collision` into the document (the box owners' models, flat breakables, the spec's cuts) and the sidecar `maps/hulls/<id>.{json,bin}` (only those hulls, from dot-map's `mesh.write_collision`, server-only like the document), and the game's server builds them as one static body (`ZzMap.build_world_collision_local`, also from the drawn world on a client). `walk_check.sh` audits with the same hulls (`--world`). A spec with `crop` or `clip_y` writes them into `world_collision` as `within` and `band`, and only the hulls touching them collide (the boxes were cropped the same way; `walk_check.sh` audits with the same region); `widen_doorways` still gets no `world_collision` and a warning (the hulls would keep the walls it trims), and no shipped map with a world uses it. **The horde's graph is the whole map's, not the wire's:** when `world_collision` is written, the importer also writes the server-only `maps/hulls/<id>.nav.json` (the boxes the budget dropped, and the floors and links of the full conversion, which the wire's 800 floors and 600 links do not limit). The game's `ZzMap.read_nav_extra` loads it and `ZzNpcs.with_nav_extra` puts it in place of the document's nav before the graph is built; a server without the file warns and walks the document's floor. Every map that dropped anything is re-imported this way (2026-10-24: the mall, the manor, both krusty krabs, roy_highway, verystill, death_star_escape and POTC are at 0.0000 lost); `zm_blockfort` and `zm_mansion_css` lost 2 brushes each (0.0023) until dot-map's `boxes.slabs` learned to cut a thin steep board (a ladder's rail, a leaning plank) finer until its slabs touch: they are at 0.0000 with no hulls. Other maps converted before that fix get the finer cut on their next re-conversion.

**`hull_collision` (spec) puts the world pack's exact hulls where the boxes were cut or cropped** (`{"cuts": [i, ...], "uncropped": true}`): `cuts` lists the `cut` regions whose hulls still collide (Run For Your Life's rock band, whose boxes seal the run and were cut for the horde's graph; the hulls are the exact rock), `uncropped` drops `within`/`band` from `world_collision` for a spec that still crops. Cuts that stand in for an opened door or a lift (Black Mesa, Nuke) are left out of it: their hulls would shut the route again. `walk_check.sh` audits with the same selection. A map under the wire budget gets an empty `nav.json` beside its hulls so the server does not warn. `walk_limit` (spec, with `walk_limit_reason`, printed by `walk_check.sh`) is a per-map walk-test limit in place of the global 0.002, for a document that cannot be re-converted: Nuke only (0.05; its grates and ladder predate the importer, re-converting splits the ground).

**Death Star Escape's 87.1% is not lost geometry** (2026-10-24, `tools/coverage_report.py`, which reads the importer's `ZZ_DROP_TRACE=path` output): of 202048 m2 drawn, 87.1% lies on a wire box, 3.9% on a box of the server's nav extra (91.0% with it, printed as completeness.py's second line), 4.7% on ceilings dropped by `drop_ceilings` and 3.9% on lids (`drop_lids`), which the world-pack hulls collide on the server and the client draws from the pack, 0.1% each on `drop_small`, the `max_boxes` cap and water, and displacements 0.0%. Putting ceilings on the wire or the nav extra would change the horde graph for nothing. `tools/camera_free.py <id> x,y,z[:lx,ly,lz]` says whether a free camera stands in a box before a frame is rendered.

**`nav_before_cap` (spec) builds the flood, route and graph from every box and applies `max_boxes` only to what the document carries**, so a map over the wire budget keeps the horde's ground of its whole conversion (Death Star Escape: no `crop`, coverage 87%, the whole station; the cap pass used to flood on the capped boxes and left 344 points and a route too short for the toys' anchors). Pin the route's end with `end_src` when `drop_small` opens a different farthest point. `drop_buried` finds containers through a grid (a whole station took 9 minutes of pair tests, now 2).

**Escape maps (exodus) need authoring the source's logic cannot carry.** Elevators, teleports, falling-rock puzzles and airstrikes have no equivalent in the map format, so a spec rewrites that part: `cut` (regions whose box centres go, optional material), `add_boxes` (stand-ins in game coordinates: a bridge, a stair whose steps are 1.0 long on the 0.5 grid and rise at most 0.5 so the metre-spaced graph has a point on each), `extra_gates` (a gate made of added boxes, at least 0.8 m thick or the route sampling misses it), `gate_names` (only these `func_door`s become gates, with their own label and hold-out), `drop_buried`, `survivor_start`/`zombie_start`/`items`, `kill_y`, `extra_links` (explicit nav links) and `route_bridge` (link the graph's components wherever the walked route crosses between them). Run `nav_emulate` on the output and read "points on the main ground" before trusting a map. Nuke and Run For Your Life ship this way; what each leaves out is in its spec.
**`head` (spec, default 1.7) is the clearance the flood requires above a cell.** A duck-under grate leaves about 1.4 m and the body crouches to 1.0, so Nuke sets 1.3; at 1.7 the route was cut at the catwalk and the toys had nowhere to anchor. The importer unions the flood's links, `extra_links` and `route_bridge` into the graph before it counts the main ground, so its "on main" figure matches what the game's `headless_maps` joins; a route point still snapped off the main component is fixed with an explicit `extra_links` entry, not a looser game check.


## Generated documents: every shippable map has one (`tools/auto_spec.py`, `ship_docs.sh`)

`tools/convert/ship.tsv` lists every map that ships (document id, store key); `tools/convert/excluded.txt` lists those that do not, with the reason. `python3 -I tools/auto_spec.py zz/<store key>` writes `tools/bsp/<id>.json` from the BSP's entities and credit (mode from the `ze_` prefix, the survivor and zombie classes the map has most of, and `ship_docs.sh` adds `terrain` when the floor is displacements) and marks it `"generated": true`; a spec without that mark is hand-tuned and never overwritten. `tools/ship_worlds.sh` then `tools/ship_docs.sh` (both take ids; run through `heavy`) write the world pack and the document, and `tools/build_credits.py` writes CREDITS.md. The importer fills what a spec cannot name: `items_auto` (a kit beside the first spawn), survivor and zombie starts on the main ground when the source's do not land on it, `max_boxes` (smallest solids go first, for the wire limit and `MAX_BOXES`). A generated exodus map has a route to the farthest reachable point and no gates: the source's logic (triggers, teleports) is not read, so tune those by hand. Not shipped: clouds (no redistribution), mars_horror (permission to alter), voodoo islands (64-point start island), adrift (2431 nav floors against 800).

## Drawn worlds (`tools/build_worlds.py`)

**A converted map's look is its own faces, not its boxes.** `python3 -I tools/build_worlds.py tools/bsp/<id>.json <bsp>` writes `world/zz_world_<id>/<id>.{json,bin}` (dot-map's `dot-bsp-mesh`, read by its `DotMapBspMesh`) and names it in the document as `"world"`. What is drawn is what the document collides with: model 0 and the spec's `solid_classes`, never a `gate_classes` model or anything that moves, minus the faces a `cut` (without a material) or `crop` drops, minus water; no hulls. `add_boxes` are not in the source, so the manifest lists them as `zz.keep_boxes` and the game keeps drawing those. Faces carry the source material name only; `zz.materials` maps it through `import_bsp.material_for`, so no texture of the author's ships. **Rerun it after every re-conversion** (a changed `cut` or `crop` changes the faces). The release's `worlds` job publishes each directory as its own pack (dot-ci's `release-maps`), which clients fetch lazily; the server-only maps pack excludes `world/`. Nuke and Run For Your Life were ingested into the store on 2026-10-24 and have worlds (Nuke's document only gained `"world"`: a re-conversion with today's importer splits its ground, 368 of 2637 points on the main one). Raven Street's source sky is a day sky (`sky_day01_09`, cyan sun) over a night map, so a document left at the importer's default environment was replaced by the game with a teal day grade and the whole street read as flat blue slabs: its spec carries an `environment` override (night, neutral sun and ambient), which makes the document differ from the default so the game keeps it; any converted night map under a day-named sky needs the same. Raven Street is in the store (zz/zs_ravenstreet_v1) with a spec key `world_crop` (game-metre rectangle outside which faces are not drawn: the distant scenery `drop_decor` removes from the boxes was drawn as dark slabs); `build_worlds.py` honours it and `regen_all.sh`/`ship_worlds.sh` call it for a spec that has it, because dot-map's regen has no crop. It is NOT in `ship.tsv` yet: a document with a world needs a block under `maps:` in game-zombie-zone's `cfg/game/game-zombie-zone/dot_weapon/spawner.yml` (`headless_maps` fails "it lays at least one weapon site" without one), and its document is the pre-shared-tool conversion, so `walk_check.sh zs_ravenstreet` fails (0.049 brushes, 0.42 floor) until it is re-converted with its next bake.

**Models a map packs get a stand-in by shape** (dot-map's `standins.shape_matches`, run by `regen` and `build_worlds.py`): a placed model with no name row in `free_models.tsv` that the pakfile carries is measured and drawn as the nearest neutral Kenney block, drum or rock at its measured size, never the author's mesh. Only maps whose share check is `none` or `ask` are re-run; the table is dot-map's, never this repository's. A packed model something rests on (a prop's origin at its top) that is no stand-in's size is drawn as a neutral block of its measured size (`box` in the manifest's props, dot-map's `_support_boxes`), so the condiments on Krusty Krab's counter do not hang in the air; the black and white generic textures take their name's colour since converter v0.1.34, so a re-run swaps `gen_flat_808080` for 000000/dfdfdf.

**`tools/regen_all.sh [outdir]` rebuilds every world pack from the lossless store** through dot-map's `bsp.py regen`, one per `ship.tsv` row (`REGEN_IDS="a b"` limits it; a row in `excluded.txt` is skipped). It finds the spec by the document id, never the store key's name, and passes its `y0` on, because a bin built without it lands y0 metres off its movers. Run it through `heavy`. **`tools/check_worlds.py` is what catches that**, on the committed files alone: each manifest is a `dot-bsp-mesh` v1 with the entity sections, its `.bin` matches the stored sha256 and size, every mover has the model its game animates (a carried solid one has a hull), every link has its five fields, the document names the pack, and the bin's hull heights agree with the movers' records (0.05 m) and the document's boxes and spawns (1 m).

**Lamps are never capped at conversion.** `import_bsp.source_lamps` keeps every `light` and `light_spot` (a spot gets `dir` and `cone`; the 120 cap is gone), and a spec's `auto_lights` grid is only for a source with under 12 lamps (`force` keeps it). A map over 128 lamps gets `wire_lamps` 32 and its lamps in farthest-point order, because the wire holds 60000 bytes and the first 32 are what a client is sent; the game lights only the nearest 128 at once. **`tools/relight.sh [--check] [ids]` refreshes only `lights` of the committed documents** (no box, nav or world drift; a map whose source has no more lamps than the document keeps its own; `--check` fails when a document would change, and `walk_check.sh` runs it). A relit map needs a rebake (`tools/bake_lightmaps.sh` in the game). 2026-10-24: 18 maps relit.

**An invisible box says what it is** (`kind` on a box with `"visible": false`, written by `import_bsp.py`'s `hidden_kind`): `clip` (player or monster clip), `nodraw` (tool textures only) or `sky` (the world's skybox). The game puts `clip` and `nodraw` on its `clip` physics layer (movers stop, shots and sight pass) and lets `sky` collide with nothing; a box with no `kind` (a document converted before 2026-10-24) is judged by height, a slab 30 m above the playable region being sky. Re-converting a map adds the word; nothing needs it.

## Toys

Every map's `toys` are dot-props' **loose** toys (barrels, tyres, balls, a bowling rack), from the builder's `TOYS` table: a hint names an anchor (the first survivor spawn, a route point, a post) and an offset, and `settle_toys` moves it to the nearest spot within 10 m floored flat, half a metre clear of every box and prop, 1.5 m from every spawn and item; a hint that settles nowhere stops the build. Never a fixed toy: the nav floors do not know about it, so it would be a wall a stand-in walks into. game-zombie-zone's `headless_maps` checks each is built, inside nothing, and still two seconds on.

## Weapons the maps name from the second pack

`molotov` and `pipe_bomb` are named as throwables. They are zed-dot-weapons' (the second pack); a server without it draws a random throwable in their place (`ZzLoot.place_from_map`).

## Publishing

**This repository is a pack of its own, mounted on the server alone.** A server owner names it in their deployment's map config (dot-server-deploy's `cfg/content.yml`), and the game adds that mount's `maps/` to its catalogue. It is never in a client's content sync: a client is sent the one document being played. `.github/workflows/release.yml` runs `build_maps.py --check` and then dot-ci's release with `pack: true`.

`project.godot` exists only so dot-ci's release can import this repository and attach the `-pack.zip`; it carries the editor's own header and no comments, because the editor rewrites the file on its first save and a checkout would show it modified.

**Publish after the game**: the Blackout maps say `exodus`, which a game before 2026-10-12 refuses.

**`provides.json` is what lists this pack in the Workshop, and `tools/build_provides.py` writes it** from the documents in the folder (an entry per map, `map_kind` and the game it is for are constants at the top). Edit the script, run it, commit both; `--check` (CI) fails on a stale file. A pack whose newest READY version has no `provides` block is not a Workshop item. It ships because the pack takes every file outside the excluded folders.

## Ambience

Every document carries `ambient` (README, `tools/ambient.py`): a converted map's comes from its spec (`"ambient"` in `tools/bsp/<id>.json`, so a re-conversion keeps it), else from the keyword vote of the world's soundscape and ambient_generic names, which is dot-audio's `DotAmbienceMoods.KEYWORDS` read from its source. The hand-built maps' moods are `ambient.HAND`, written by `build_maps.py`. `python3 -I tools/ambient.py --check` fails a document without one.

## solid_props

A drawn world's solid props (`solid` 2, 3, 4 or 6 in the manifest) are boxes in the document's `solid_props`, written by `tools/solid_props.sh` (it starts Godot: run under `heavy`, `GAME=` names the game checkout whose `maps`/`world` links point here). `regen_all.sh`, `ship_worlds.sh` and `ship_docs.sh` run it; the importer keeps the previous block and `settle_toys` keeps toys out of the boxes; `check_worlds.py` fails a world with solid props and no block. `solid_props.sh` links the game's `.stores` to this repository's (a world's licence names `../../.stores/model-library`, which from `res://world/<pack>` is the game's own): without it no stand-in loads and every document silently gets 0 boxes.
